# Cosmic Journey

Build an Immersive Animated Website:

The Worlds of Buddhism

Create a visually spectacular, highly interactive educational website that allows visitors to travel through the worlds, realms, states of existence, and cosmological structures described across Buddhist traditions.

The experience should feel less like reading a conventional website and more like journeying through a living Buddhist cosmology.

The central interaction is:

Scroll → Travel → Enter a Realm → Explore → Understand → Continue the Journey

The aesthetic should combine:

Buddhist sacred art

Himalayan landscapes

ancient manuscripts

mandalas

temple architecture

cosmic astronomy

ethereal particle systems

cinematic environmental design

modern museum-quality information design

subtle psychedelic/surreal visual language

The website must remain educational and respectful rather than presenting every cosmological description as scientifically established fact. Clearly identify traditions and interpretations where they differ.

1. Core Concept

Build the entire website around an interactive vertical journey through Buddhist cosmology.

The visitor begins in an enormous cosmic environment.

A central message slowly appears:

The Worlds of Buddhism

A Journey Through Buddhist Cosmology

Below it:

Explore the realms of existence, consciousness, suffering, awakening, and liberation described across Buddhist traditions.

CTA:

Begin the Journey ↓

When clicked, the camera should smoothly descend into the cosmological visualization.

2. The Main Experience

Do not create ordinary page-to-page navigation.

Instead, create a continuous cinematic journey.

As users scroll, the environment transforms.

Use techniques such as:

scroll-linked animation

parallax depth

camera movement

crossfades

particle transitions

fog

clouds

light rays

stars

floating Sanskrit/Pali characters used sparingly

animated mandalas

depth-of-field effects

subtle environmental movement

layered landscapes

portals between realms

smooth color-temperature changes

ambient gradients

3D-like depth

Transitions should take approximately 800–1800 ms depending on context and use elegant easing.

Never abruptly switch scenes.

Every realm should appear to emerge naturally from the previous one.

4. Opening — The Wheel of Becoming

Introduce the concept of Saṃsāra.

Create a slowly rotating cosmic wheel inspired by the Bhavacakra without simply copying a specific copyrighted illustration.

Explain:

Saṃsāra

rebirth

karma

dependent origination

impermanence

suffering

liberation

Show the traditional Six Realms as an interactive circular visualization.

Each segment becomes illuminated when hovered.

Clicking one begins the journey into that realm.

5. Realm I — Naraka

Hell Realms

Transition downward.

The warm cosmic environment becomes darker.

Particles resemble embers.

Mountains and distant landscapes emerge through atmospheric haze.

Present Naraka as realms traditionally associated with extreme suffering resulting from karmic conditions.

Create sections for:

Hot Hells

Explore traditional descriptions such as:

Sañjīva

Kālasūtra

Saṃghāta

Raurava

Mahāraurava

Tapana

Pratāpana

Avīci

Cold Hells

Represent these with enormous frozen landscapes, snow, mist, cracking ice, and distant blue-white mountains.

Important:

Avoid graphic violence.

Represent suffering symbolically through environments, isolation, heat, cold, scale, sound design cues, and abstraction.

Include:

What does Naraka mean philosophically?

Discuss literal cosmological, psychological, ethical, and metaphorical interpretations found among Buddhist traditions and modern interpreters.

6. Realm II — Preta

The Realm of Hungry Ghosts

Visual motifs:

enormous empty landscapes

distant lights

Explain the concept of Preta.

craving

attachment

A glowing object continuously moves away as the cursor approaches it.

Caption:

Craving promises arrival while continually moving the destination.

7. Realm III — Animal Realm

The barren environment gradually transforms into forests, oceans, mountains, insects, birds, mammals, and marine environments.

Create a beautiful living ecosystem.

Discuss Buddhist perspectives concerning:

Create subtle animated wildlife silhouettes.

Keep the experience contemplative rather than documentary-like.

8. Realm IV — Human Realm

The camera rises toward sunrise.

Cities, villages, monasteries, forests, homes, roads, birth, ageing, relationships, work, illness, celebration and death appear through layered silhouettes.

Title:

The Human Realm

The Realm of Rare Opportunity

Explain why human existence is frequently regarded in Buddhist traditions as especially valuable for Dharma practice.

Explore:

Birth → Childhood → Youth → Adulthood → Ageing → Death → ?

As users move through it, the figures gradually transform.

10. Realm VI — Deva

The storm disappears.

The visitor rises above the clouds.

Introduce enormous luminous celestial landscapes.

Use:

floating architecture

lotus forms

distant celestial structures

subtle halos

enormous scale

Title:

Deva

The Heavenly Realms

Explain that Buddhist heavens are generally not eternal destinations.

Even extraordinarily pleasurable states remain within Saṃsāra.

Highlight:

Even heaven is impermanent.

Explore major heavenly classifications relevant to Buddhist cosmology, clearly identifying the textual/traditional framework being used.

11. Mount Meru

Create a stylized cosmological diagram showing:

12. The Desire Realm

Introduce:

Kāmadhātu

The Realm of Desire

Show how multiple forms of existence belong to the Desire Realm.

Create an animated vertical diagram showing relevant cosmological levels.

Explain how craving and sensory attachment characterize this domain.

13. The Form Realm

Continue upward.

Matter becomes increasingly abstract.

Mountains dissolve into geometry.

Architecture dissolves into light.

Title:

Rūpadhātu

The Realm of Form

Explore the Brahmā worlds and their relationship in Buddhist cosmological systems to meditative absorptions or jhānas/dhyānas.

Create multiple floating planes representing increasingly refined states.

The visitor should feel that the physical universe is gradually disappearing.

14. The Formless Realm

The interface itself begins disappearing.

Navigation becomes translucent.

The background becomes almost pure space.

Title:

Arūpadhātu

The Formless Realm

Explore the four commonly described formless attainments:

Infinite Space

Visual: stars expand endlessly.

Infinite Consciousness

The stars transform into points of awareness.

Nothingness

Almost everything disappears.

Neither Perception Nor Non-Perception

The interface becomes extraordinarily minimal and ambiguous.

Use typography and motion rather than literal imagery.

15. But This Is Still Saṃsāra

After the visitor reaches the apparent highest point, display:

And Yet…

Pause the animation.

Then reveal:

None of these worlds is liberation.

Explain that even extremely refined cosmological or meditative states are distinguished from final liberation in Buddhist thought.

The entire cosmological structure slowly becomes visible below the visitor.

16. Nirvāṇa

Do not depict Nirvāṇa simply as another physical world at the top of the hierarchy.

Instead, visually break the cosmological interface.

The cosmic map disappears.

The hierarchy dissolves.

Particles stop.

The screen becomes extremely calm.

Title:

Nirvāṇa

Subtitle:

Beyond the Cycle

Explain carefully that Buddhist traditions generally resist treating Nirvāṇa as merely another heavenly location.

Introduce concepts including:

cessation

liberation

freedom from craving

freedom from ignorance

ending the cycle of rebirth

awakening

Use restrained animation.

This should be the quietest section of the entire website.

24. Meditation Mode

Include a global button:

When activated:

disable distracting animations

slow environmental movement

remove explanatory cards

leave the current realm visible

optionally enable subtle ambient audio

show a minimal breathing indicator

Users can simply remain inside a realm contemplatively.

33. Accessibility

Respect:

37. Important Design Principle

The website must not feel like:

Homepage → Section → Section → Footer

It should feel like:

Universe → Descent → Saṃsāra → Realms → Ascent → Higher States → Dissolution → Liberation

Scrolling should create the sensation that the visitor is physically travelling through a vast cosmological structure.

The interface should become progressively more minimal as the visitor ascends.

At the beginning there is form, movement and complexity.

At the end there is space, stillness and simplicity.

38. Build Priority

For the first working version, prioritize these experiences:

Cinematic landing experience

Scroll-driven realm transitions

Six Realms

Interactive Cosmic Map

Mount Meru visualization

Form and Formless Realms

Nirvāṇa transition

Wheel of Life Explorer

Responsive mobile experience

Meditation Mode

Create these first before adding secondary educational features.

Final Creative Direction

Treat this project as if an international Buddhist studies museum, a world-class interactive design studio, and a cinematic VFX team collaborated to build an online experience about Buddhist cosmology.

It should be:

Beautiful without becoming kitsch.

Psychedelic without becoming chaotic.

Spiritual without becoming preachy.

Scholarly without becoming dry.

Cinematic without becoming unusable.

Above all, make the transition between worlds the defining feature of the experience.

The visitor should feel that they have not merely read about Buddhist cosmology.

They have travelled through it.
